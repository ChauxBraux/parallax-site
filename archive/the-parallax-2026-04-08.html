U.S. and Iran Agree to Two-Week Ceasefire, Averting Escalation Hours Before Trump's Deadline

The United States and Iran reached a two-week ceasefire agreement on Tuesday, barely one hour before President Trump's self-imposed 8 p.m. ET deadline to launch massive strikes on Iranian infrastructure. Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif and Field Marshal Asim Munir brokered the proposal, which Trump credited in his announcement. As part of the agreement, Iran pledged to allow safe passage through the Strait of Hormuz under coordination with its armed forces, while the U.S. and Israel agreed to suspend bombing operations. Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi confirmed Tehran's acceptance and stated the deal was based on Iran's 10-point plan; Iran's Supreme National Security Council declared the agreement a "victory." Peace talks are expected to begin Friday in Islamabad, Pakistan.
